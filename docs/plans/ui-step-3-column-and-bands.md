# UI step 3: the full-height column and the band heights

Third step of the UI roadmap (`docs/plans/ui-roadmap.md`): the primary side bar, with the activity bar when it is shown, becomes the outer column of the workbench grid from the top window edge to the bottom one, and the title row and the status line get their final heights, so that every later step styles against the final geometry. Decided by the maintainer on 2026-10-20: the title band is 44px and the status line 34px at the default density, both keep upstream's heights at the compact density until the design provides them, and Windows and Linux keep a full-width title bar until a design for them exists.

## The mockup

Measured headlessly in `VSebCode v10.dc.html` (window 1556 × 912): the glass column spans the full height, with an empty 44px band at the top (the lights are drawn at y 16 to 28, centered in it) and the project header and the tree below it down to the bottom edge. The main area beside it holds a 44px title row, the editor cards and a 34px status line whose 22px pills are centered (6px above and below). The title row content starts 14px right of the column edge; with the column hidden, and in zen, it starts 86px from the window edge (the main area's 10px padding plus 76px), 16px clear of the lights. Zen hides the status line. The mockup has no full screen, no activity bar, no right-hand side bar, no banner and no panel other than the bottom terminal drawer, which sits in the main area.

## Two grid shapes

- Column with a band: the macOS desktop with the custom title bar (every glass window). The root is horizontal, `[activity bar, side bar, main]`; main is vertical, `[title bar, banner, middle row, status bar]`.
- Column below the title: the native title bar on macOS (which `window.nativeTabs` and `window.nativeFullScreen: false` also force), Windows and Linux. The root is vertical, `[title bar, body]`; body is horizontal, `[activity bar, side bar, main]`; main is vertical, `[banner, middle row, status bar]`. The title bar keeps upstream's height, menu bar and window controls; the banner stays in the main area as the roadmap places it.
- In both shapes a side bar on the right mirrors the column (`[main, side bar, activity bar]`), the middle row holds the editor, the panel and the secondary side bar, and the status line spans the main area only. The shape follows the title bar style, which only changes with a restart, so a window never switches shape. The web-only banner-first order is not part of either desktop shape.
- The panel can no longer reach under the primary side bar: panel alignment keeps its meaning for the secondary side bar, and for the primary side bar every alignment behaves like `center`. Panel maximization keeps upstream's restriction to `center`.
- The middle row keeps the editor and the secondary side bar (shown or not) as direct children in every state, because the grid flattens a branch left with one child, which would move every location below it. Alignment therefore moves the panel instead of the secondary side bar: a horizontal panel that may reach under the secondary side bar (`justify`, or the alignment on that bar's side) sits in main above or below the middle row; otherwise it shares a column with the editor inside the row, and a vertical panel sits beside the editor. Each arrangement looks like upstream's for the same alignment.

## The column

- Band: in the first shape the column parts reserve a top band as tall as the title row (zero while the title row is hidden, as in full screen), so their content starts level with the editor. The band is a window drag region (one element the layout service keeps over the column's band, like the glass backing), so the column edge is not resizable there, as the title row is not today. The lights stay where upstream places them, vertically centered in the title row's height through `updateWindowControls`, which centers them in the band. A change of the title row's height or visibility lays the column parts out again explicitly, because the grid skips a leaf whose bounds did not change (step 1).
- Title row inset: while the lights sit over the title row (no column on the left, the column on the right, zen out of full screen) its content starts no earlier than 68px right of the lights' left edge, which is the mockup's 86px for lights at 18px, minus the width of a column on the left (the activity bar alone covers part of them). The inset is a variable on the main container that the title bar applies as its leading padding; step 4 combines it with the title row's own padding. Zero in full screen and in the second shape. In right-to-left locales, where macOS puts the lights on the right, the title row keeps upstream's controls container there and gets no leading inset.
- Surface (Q1, Q2): the column is flush with the window edges and the main area in every state, as the mockup draws it: glass where step 1 applies, otherwise the opaque side bar color with a 1px inner edge in `sideBar.border`, the mockup's reduced-transparency and solid fallbacks. The activity bar, when shown, is a flush rail with its own background over the full height, its icons below the band. Modern UI's floating cards (editor, panel, secondary side bar) live in the main area, and its floating-card helpers treat the column as outside their cluster.
- Glass: the backing's hole stays the side bar's rectangle, which now includes the band; the activity bar stays opaque (step 1 scope).

## The heights

| Band | Default density, Modern UI on | Compact density, or Modern UI off |
|---|---|---|
| Title row and column band, first shape | 44px | upstream: 35px with the command center, otherwise 32px on macOS 26 and later, 28px before |
| Title row, second shape | upstream | upstream |
| Status line, both shapes | the item height (22px at the default status bar font size) plus 12px, items centered: 34px | upstream: the item height plus 4px at compact density, plus nothing without Modern UI |

- Only the main window's title bar and status bar change; auxiliary windows keep upstream's. The two layout computations that read title or status bar geometry for any window, `computeContainerOffset` (the quick input's top in every window) and the auxiliary branch of `getMaximumEditorDimensions`, use that window's own parts, their own visibility (an auxiliary window decides its title bar per its own full screen state and hides its status bar when compact, `auxiliaryEditorPart.ts:208-250`, while `isVisible` answers for the main window) and the banner only in the main window, which owns it.
- Window controls: each window kind keeps its own cached control height (main windows keep today's key, auxiliary windows get their own), so a new window's lights start where its own title row will put them instead of where the last reporting window's did; the macOS sheet offset, where native dialogs drop from, follows each window's reported title row height instead of the fixed 32px or 28px.

## Other consumers of the band heights

- Notifications: the toasts' and the notification center's anchoring (Modern UI's `--modern-ui-notifications-block-start-inset` and `--modern-ui-notifications-block-end-inset`) and their height budgets (today 22px reserved per visible title or status bar in `notificationsToasts.ts` and `notificationsCenter.ts`) follow the visible title row and status line, so a tall toast or the center never crosses a band; step 12 restyles them.
- Floating debug toolbar: its vertical range (today a fixed 28px or 35px for the title bar) follows the active window's title row and banner, and is recomputed when they change (density, zoom, full screen), so its default place stays below the title row; step 4 adds the title row's own debug pills.
- Keyboard part navigation (`getVisibleNeighborPart`) skips the title bar, banner and status bar, which in the first shape border the column on the main area's side, so moving right from the side bar still reaches the editor.
- The splash saves the window's shape and the title row and status line rectangles; the painting moves out of `workbench.ts` into a function of `code/electron-browser/workbench/partsSplash.ts` that returns the painted rectangles, so tests can call it. It paints the column at full height (with the glass hole), the title row over the main area and the status line under it, with and without saved part bounds.
- Zen mode, the maximized secondary side bar, the maximized panel, the hidden side bar (an empty column) and full screen go through the existing visibility code; the captures check each.

## Review

Adversarial review of the first draft, 2026-10-20, verdict "needs attention", five medium findings, all verified against the code and folded in; none rejected. (1) `computeContainerOffset` and the auxiliary branch of `getMaximumEditorDimensions` (`layout.ts:235-247`, `1295-1300`) take the main window's title and status bars for every window, so an auxiliary window's quick input would start at 44px under a 32px title bar; both now read the target window's parts. (2) The notification toasts and center reserve a fixed 22px per visible band (`notificationsToasts.ts:643-652`, `notificationsCenter.ts:434-443`), so moving only their anchors would let tall notifications cross the 44px and 34px bands; their budgets now follow the bands. (3) The floating debug toolbar clamps its place to a fixed 28px or 35px (`debugToolBar.ts:307-323`) and recomputes the range only on tab or command center changes (`:150-157`), which would put it across the 44px title row; it is now in scope. (4) Main and auxiliary windows share one cached control height (`windowImpl.ts:232-238`, `486-499`; `AuxiliaryWindow` extends `BaseWindow`), so after a 44px main window an auxiliary window's lights would start 6px off; each window kind now keeps its own. (5) The second shape put the banner across the full width, which the roadmap does not allow (it places the banner in the main area and only exempts the title bar); the banner now sits in the main area in both shapes. The review's next steps, tests on real parts, the side bar's relayout and a callable splash painter, are in the test list. The main session also replaced the draft's tree-equality test of the grid with box checks, because the grid flattens a branch left with one child after a move, and added the right-to-left case.
